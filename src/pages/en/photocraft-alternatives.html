Photopea for Another Browser-Based PSD Workflow

Photopea’s official site describes a browser editor with layers, masks, and PSD opening and saving. It is a candidate when you want to remain in the browser but need to compare how a particular PSD is handled.

Try a copy of the same document in both editors. Check dimensions, layer names, editable text, masks and the flattened output after saving. Official format support does not guarantee that every effect in your document will match. Photopea has its own service terms and privacy behavior; our PhotoCraft network observations do not apply to it.

GIMP for an Installed Open-Source Photo Editor

GIMP is a free, open-source desktop image editor for systems including Windows, macOS and GNU/Linux. Its official site describes retouching, compositing and extensibility through scripts and plugins.

Consider it when desktop photo work and control over an installed editor matter more than keeping PhotoCraft’s interface. Expect to learn a different workflow. Confirm the formats and plugins needed for your actual task; do not treat an imported PSD as proof of a lossless Photoshop round trip.

Krita for Painting and Illustration

Krita is a free, open-source painting program. Its official feature overview includes brush types, layers, drawing assistants, stabilizers and animation tools.

It is worth examining when drawing and brush control are the main job. Test a small piece of your own artwork with your tablet, then export it in the required format. A painting-focused editor should be assessed against your art workflow rather than assumed to replace every photo-production tool.

Photoshop for an Existing Photoshop Production Workflow

Adobe Photoshop is an option when collaborators already use it or your work depends on its native documents, specific plugins or documented features. Adobe provides paid plans and trial offers; check current access and regional terms.

Keep the required fonts, linked assets and plugins with your test. Native format use alone does not solve missing project resources. Our PhotoCraft vs Photoshop comparison separates official Adobe information from the small PhotoCraft workflow we tested.

PhotoCraft Desktop When the Browser Is the Limitation

You may not need a different product. The upstream desktop app can avoid browser-specific requirements, although it does not guarantee a fix for every issue. Its release and feature set can differ from our hosted WebAssembly build.

Choose the package for your system in the desktop download guide, and verify its source and checksum. We checked release filenames, not installation on every operating system.

A Small Test Before You Move Your Work

  1. Write down the task that PhotoCraft cannot finish for you and the output you need.
  2. Make a copy of one representative document. Record dimensions, layers, fonts and any effects that must remain editable.
  3. Run the same edit in the candidate editor. Save its editable document and export the delivery image separately.
  4. Reopen both outputs. Check appearance and editability, then decide whether the new workflow solves the original problem.

For a simple starting file, use our PNG and two-layer PSD tutorial. Add representative text, transparency or effects only when those are part of your real work.

PhotoCraft Alternatives FAQ

Which PhotoCraft alternative is best?

There is no single winner for all of these tasks. Choose a candidate from the table, then verify it with your own document.

Are all these options free and open source?

No. GIMP and Krita are open-source projects; Photopea’s site offers free browser editing; Adobe’s plans have their own paid and trial terms. These are different access models.

Can an alternative preserve every PSD layer and effect?

This page makes no such guarantee. Check a saved and reopened sample in the exact versions you plan to use.

Use this PhotoCraft alternatives shortlist to solve a specific workflow gap. If the tested tutorial already completes your task, you can keep using PhotoCraft in the browser.
