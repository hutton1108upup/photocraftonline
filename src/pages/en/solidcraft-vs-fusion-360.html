Choose by the job you need to finish

For learning parametric modeling, making simple parts or exploring MCP automation, SolidCraft is worth evaluating with a small case. If delivery depends on established machining, simulation, drawing or team-data workflows, keep your validated toolchain while testing each migration requirement.

A familiar interface, assembly commands or STEP export do not establish equivalent functionality, geometry robustness or file compatibility. This comparison covers documented scope. Our executed case is the mounting plate, not a performance benchmark between the two applications.

Run a one-part migration trial

  1. Choose a simple, shareable copy of a part and record dimensions, units, hole positions and deliverable formats.
  2. Open or rebuild it in SolidCraft, then edit a dimension that affects later features.
  3. Export STEP and open it in the receiving application. Check volume, units, body count and critical dimensions.
  4. If drawings, CAM or simulation are required, validate those workflows separately before deciding to migrate. STEP success alone does not validate the whole toolchain.

When should you consider CADCraft?

For primarily 2D technical drawings and command-driven drafting, start with CADCraft and CADCraft vs AutoCAD. For 3D parts with sketches, extrusions and dimension-driven history, continue with the SolidCraft tutorial.
